## Identified conflicts

- Legacy documents used multiple current identities, including historical OT-focused names, Clinical Assistant Platform language, and Clinical Continuity Platform language. The active stack resolves current identity as Clinical Continuity Platform within the future Continuity Platform direction while preserving old documents in the archive.
- Some legacy roadmap language treated Longitudinal Visibility / Continuity Compression as future opportunity, while current implementation evidence includes continuity compression capabilities. Active documents avoid describing continuity compression as merely future.
- Legacy architecture/status documents sometimes imply broad platform reuse. Active documents label shared architecture as candidate and require validation before universal claims.
- Historical validation documents contain strong simulated findings. Active documents preserve the boundary that simulation does not establish real clinician proof.

## Unresolved gaps

- Minimum Shared Continuity Foundation remains unclassified component by component.

- Real-clinician validation, pilot readiness, and production readiness remain unestablished.
