# Clinical Attention Model

---

# Purpose

This document defines how longitudinal clinical information becomes actionable clinician attention.

The purpose of the system is not merely to track progression.

The purpose of the system is to help clinicians determine:

What requires attention today?

Clinical Attention serves as the bridge between:

- progression
- continuity
- reassessment
- operational prioritization
- treatment execution

---

# Core Principle

Clinicians do not primarily open a case to review progression.

Clinicians open a case to determine:

- what changed
- what matters
- what requires attention
- what should be addressed today

Progression exists to support clinical attention.

Clinical attention exists to support treatment decisions.

---

# Clinical Attention Hierarchy

Barrier Evolution
↓
Functional Change
↓
Milestone Achievement
↓
Clinical Attention Required
↓
Operational Prioritization
↓
Treatment Focus

---

# Definition

Clinical Attention Required represents the most important issue currently affecting occupational performance, safety, participation, caregiver burden, or progression.

Examples:

- Unable to complete bathing due to endurance limitations
- Transfer consistency remains unsafe
- Caregiver confidence declining
- Fall risk remains elevated
- Medication management errors increasing

---

# Sources Of Clinical Attention

## Barrier Evolution

Examples:

- endurance becomes dominant barrier
- safety awareness emerges as primary limitation
- caregiver dependence remains unresolved

## Functional Change

Examples:

- bathing improved
- transfers declined
- mobility unchanged

## Milestone Achievement

Examples:

- safe shower transfer achieved
- supervision level achieved
- independent task completion achieved

## Reassessment Signals

Examples:

- repeated falls
- hospitalization
- caregiver disruption
- plateau with unresolved safety concerns

# Attention Categories

- Function
- Safety

---

# Attention Statement Characteristics

Effective clinical attention statements should be:

- actionable
- concise
- clinically meaningful
- understandable in seconds

Poor Example:

Endurance is the dominant barrier.

Better Example:

Patient unable to complete bathing routine due to endurance limitations.

---

# Relationship To Operational Prioritization

Clinical Attention answers:

What requires attention today?

Operational Prioritization answers:

What should treatment focus on?

Example:

Barrier:
Endurance

Clinical Attention:
Unable to complete bathing routine due to endurance limitations

Operational Priority:
Occupational Endurance Development

Treatment Focus:
Activity tolerance during bathing tasks

---

# Relationship To Progression

Progression informs clinical attention.

The system should not surface progression for its own sake.

The system should surface progression when it changes what deserves clinician attention.

---

# UX Governance

The command center should prioritize:

1. Current Clinical Attention Required
2. Current Operational Emphasis
3. Most Recent Progression Event
4. Current Functional State

Clinical attention should be visible immediately upon opening a case.

---

# Explicit Confirmation Requirements

The clinician should explicitly confirm:

- treatment direction changed
- dominant limitation changed
- reassessment escalation

The system may recommend attention shifts.

The clinician remains the final authority.

---

# Success Criteria

A successful Clinical Attention Model allows a clinician to answer within seconds:

- What changed?
- What matters?
- What requires my attention today?
- Should treatment focus change?
- Do I need reassessment?

without reviewing the full evaluation, intake, or longitudinal history.
