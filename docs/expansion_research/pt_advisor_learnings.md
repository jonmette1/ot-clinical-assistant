# PT Advisor Learnings

Last Updated: 2026-06-03

---

# Purpose

This document captures key findings from PT-oriented evaluation of the OT Clinical Assistant platform.

Unlike prior expansion research documents, this document focuses on:

* perceived value
* workflow fit
* adoption considerations
* strategic positioning
* clinical resonance

The purpose is to identify what a practicing Home Health PT finds compelling, concerning, and valuable about the platform.

This document does not represent validated market research.

It represents advisor-level directional findings intended to inform future PT exploration.

---

# Executive Summary

The strongest finding is that PT clinicians appear to recognize the underlying workflow immediately.

The evaluation suggests that the platform's value is not primarily:

* documentation
* treatment planning
* compliance support
* note generation

Instead, PT clinicians consistently describe value in:

* orientation
* continuity
* change detection
* prioritization
* clinical context reconstruction

The PT workflow appears highly compatible with the platform architecture.

The primary challenge is not workflow fit.

The primary challenge is ensuring that PT-specific mobility information drives the workflow outputs.

---

# Core Findings

---

## Finding 1

### The Continuity Problem Exists In PT

The evaluator repeatedly identified continuity as a major pain point.

Examples included:

* reconstructing patient status
* reviewing prior notes
* determining what changed
* identifying progression
* preparing before entering the home

The workflow appears directly aligned with those challenges.

Implication:

The continuity problem is not unique to OT.

This increases confidence that the platform addresses a broader rehabilitation workflow issue.

---

## Finding 2

### Command Center Workflow Translates Naturally

The evaluator strongly identified with:

* Current Status
* Since Last Visit
* Attention Required
* Next Action

These sections closely mirror how experienced PTs prepare for visits.

Implication:

The Command Center architecture appears discipline-agnostic.

The workflow survived translation better than expected.

---

## Finding 3

### Clinical Reconstruction Is The Real Problem

One of the strongest observations:

> The challenge is not finding information. The challenge is integrating information.

The evaluator described spending time mentally reconstructing:

* mobility status
* progression
* caregiver involvement
* environmental barriers
* treatment priorities

Implication:

The product may be solving a cognition problem rather than a documentation problem.

---

## Finding 4

### The Product Is Viewed As An Interpretation Layer

The evaluator consistently distinguished:

### EMR

Stores information.

### Clinical Assistant

Interprets information.

This distinction appeared intuitive.

Implication:

The product may be better positioned as a clinical intelligence layer than as a documentation tool.

---

## Finding 5

### The Product Competes With Chart Reconstruction

The evaluator did not view:

* HCHB
* WellSky
* Axxess
* Net Health
* MatrixCare

as primary competitors.

Instead, the perceived competition was:

* chart review
* note reconstruction
* clinician memory
* pre-visit preparation effort

Implication:

The product category may be different from traditional clinical software categories.

# What Resonated Most

---

## Rapid Orientation

The evaluator strongly valued:

* understanding current status
* identifying primary limitation
* recognizing safety concerns
* understanding recent change

within approximately one minute.

This appears to be one of the highest-value use cases.

---

## Cognitive Compression

The evaluator repeatedly responded positively to:

* summarization
* prioritization
* continuity support

rather than information storage.

The perceived value comes from reducing cognitive workload.

---

## Operational Priorities

The evaluator emphasized:

> "What is the bottleneck to progression?"

rather than:

> "What are all the problems?"

Implication:

Operational prioritization appears highly aligned with PT thinking.

---

## Clinical Impact Summary

The evaluator strongly valued explanations of:

* what changed
* why it matters
* treatment implications

This aligns closely with the current architecture.

---

# What Appears Missing

---

## Objective Mobility Progression

The most consistent gap identified was visibility into measurable mobility change.

Desired examples:

* gait distance trend
* transfer assistance trend
* stair performance trend
* endurance trend
* balance trend
* fall history trend

Implication:

PTs place significant trust in measurable progression.

This may represent the most important PT-specific enhancement area.

---

# Adoption Risks

---

## Double Documentation

This was identified as the largest adoption risk.

The evaluator stated that requiring clinicians to:

1. document in the EMR
2. document again in the platform

would significantly reduce adoption.

Implication:

The platform's value must exceed maintenance burden.

---

## Insufficient Cognitive Return

The evaluator implicitly validated the following hypothesis:

Additional effort is acceptable if it creates meaningful cognitive savings.

The key question becomes:

> Does 5% additional effort save 25-50% of cognitive reconstruction effort?

If yes:

Adoption becomes plausible.

If no:

Adoption becomes difficult.

---

## Trust

The evaluator highlighted the need for:

* source visibility
* transparent reasoning
* longitudinal consistency
* measurable progression

Trust appears closely tied to explainability.

---

# Emerging Strategic Insights

---

## Clinical Attention Is The Scarce Resource

One of the strongest themes emerging from the evaluation:

The product may not be optimizing documentation.

The product may be optimizing clinical attention.

Potential framing:

> Helping clinicians allocate attention more effectively between visits.

---

## The Product Is A Clinical Operating Layer

The evaluator's interpretation evolved from:

### Initial View

Treatment planning platform.

To:

### Final View

Continuity-aware clinical operating system.

This is a notable shift.

Implication:

The strategic positioning may be stronger than originally assumed.

---

## The Product Solves A Cognition Problem

The evaluator repeatedly described:

* rebuilding context
* reconstructing patient reality
* integrating fragmented information

This suggests the platform's primary value may be cognitive rather than administrative.

# Key Conclusions

1. PT clinicians appear to recognize the continuity problem immediately.

2. Command Center architecture appears highly transferable.

3. The product is perceived as an interpretation system rather than a documentation system.

4. The strongest value proposition is reducing clinical reconstruction effort.

5. Objective mobility progression visibility appears to be the largest PT-specific gap.

6. Double documentation remains the largest adoption risk.

7. Current evidence increases confidence that the platform may represent a broader rehabilitation continuity framework rather than an OT-only workflow solution.

---

# Recommended Next Question

The next strategic question is not:

> Can PT use the workflow?

Current evidence suggests yes.

The next strategic question is:

> Can PT-specific semantics be translated into the platform with sufficiently low effort that PT advisors evaluate the workflow rather than dismissing it as OT software?

Answering that question should be the focus of future PT prototype exploration.
